Every page shows one map, a few headline numbers and two rankings. The overview page combines all repositories, counting each person once even if they starred several of them.

The numbers

Stars
The star count GitHub shows on the repository.
Stargazers read
How many of those stargazers could be listed. It can be lower than the star count when accounts have been deleted or GitHub limits the listing.
Unique stargazers
On the overview page: people who starred at least one repository, each counted once.
Stargazers located
Stargazers whose profile has a location that could be found on a map, with their share of the total.
Countries
Countries with at least one located stargazer.

Map layers

Use the layer control in the top-right corner of the map to switch each layer on or off. Your choice is remembered on the next page you open.

  • Countries are shaded by their number of stargazers, darker for more. The scale is logarithmic, so a country with 1,000 stargazers doesn't drown out one with 10. The legend in the bottom-left corner gives the values. Hover a country to see its count.
  • Bubbles mark places (cities, regions, city states). A bubble's area grows with its number of stargazers. Click one to see the place and its count.

Merged bubbles

When two bubbles in the same country sit almost on top of each other, they merge into one at the current zoom level. Hover a merged bubble to see its biggest places, and click it to zoom in until it splits. Bubbles in different countries never merge.

Someone whose location is only a country ("Germany") is drawn as a bubble at the centre of that country, marked no city given. They count towards the country, but don't appear in "Top places".

Top countries and top places

The two tables under the map list the fifteen countries and places with the most stargazers. Bars use the same colours as the map: blue for countries, orange for places. Places with different names but the same geocoded label (say, "SF" and "San Francisco") are counted as one.
